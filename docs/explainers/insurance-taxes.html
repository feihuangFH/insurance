How much do taxes add to home insurance premiums?

The taxes and levies on home insurance in each state, what the Insurance Council claims, and what regulators and parliamentary inquiries have found about taxes and rising premiums.

Short answer

GST of 10% applies to home insurance across Australia. Every state and territory except the ACT also charges stamp duty of 9% to 11%, generally calculated on the premium including GST. In NSW, an Emergency Services Levy adds an average of 18% to residential premiums before GST and duty are charged. These taxes are a percentage of the premium, so the tax paid rises whenever the premium rises. The ACCC found that higher natural peril risk was the main reason insurers’ costs were higher in northern Australia.

Which taxes apply

GST. A flat 10% GST applies to all general insurance products across Australia1, p. 54.

Stamp duty. States and territories charge duty on insurance premiums. The duty is generally calculated on the premium including GST, so the two taxes compound1, p. 55.

Emergency services levies. NSW funds its fire and emergency services through a levy. Insurance companies pay 73.7% of it, local councils 11.7% and the NSW Government 14.6%. Insurers pass their share on to policies such as home, contents and car insurance9, p. 1. NSW Treasury estimates that the levy adds an average of 18% to residential premiums and 34% to non-residential premiums9, p. 5. The levy is added to the base premium, so it also increases the amount on which GST and stamp duty are charged9, p. 3.

The NSW Government states that all other mainland states fund emergency services through levies on property10. Victoria, for example, removed its fire services levy from insurance premiums on 1 July 2013 and moved it to council rates11.

How the taxes add up

The ACCC calculated the combined effect of GST and stamp duty in northern Australia in 2020.

Combined tax rate Tax as a share of the retail premium
Queensland 19.9% 16.6%
Western Australia 21% 17.4%
Northern Territory 21% 17.4%

The combined tax rate is the amount added to the premium the insurer charges. The share of the retail premium is the part of the final price that goes in tax1, p. 55.

In 2018–19, customers in northern Australia paid an average of $413 in tax on a combined home and contents policy, made up of $207 in stamp duty and $206 in GST. In the rest of Australia, the average was $256, including levies1, p. 55.

Worked example: NSW

In NSW, the Emergency Services Levy is added first, and GST and stamp duty are then charged on top of it9, p. 3. The example below applies the published rates to an insurer’s price of $100. It uses the average levy of 18%9, p. 5, GST of 10% and stamp duty of 9%2.

Step Price
Insurer’s price $100.00
Add the levy (18% of $100.00) $118.00
Add GST (10% of $118.00) $129.80
Add stamp duty (9% of $129.80) $141.48

In this example, levies and taxes add $41.48, or about 41%, to the insurer’s price. They make up about 29% of the $141.48 the customer pays. This is an illustration. The levy varies between properties and insurers, so the amount on any one policy will differ.

What the Insurance Council says

The Insurance Council of Australia has published several figures on state insurance taxes.

  • 10 to 30%. Its 2025 fact pack calls for governments to “remove state taxes to immediately drop the price of insurance by 10 to 30%”. It reports that customers paid $8.6 billion in state taxes on insurance in 2023–2412.
  • 9 to 40%. Its February 2026 pre-budget submission states that state taxes and charges make up between 9% and 40% of insurance premiums, depending on the state. It reports that states and territories raised more than $8.9 billion from levies and duties on insurance premiums in 2024–2513, p. 9.
  • 20 to 40%. Its tax reform campaign page states that government taxes and charges “can add 20 to 40 per cent” to the cost of a premium, depending on the state or territory14.

The three figures are expressed differently. The first is a fall in price, the second a share of premiums and the third an addition to the cost of a premium. The publications do not set out how each range was calculated.

Do taxes explain rising premiums?

Taxes rise with premiums. Stamp duty is a percentage of the premium, so as premiums grow, the dollar amount of stamp duty grows too1, p. 60. Total GST and stamp duty collected on home, contents and strata insurance in northern Australia rose from $48.5 million in 2007–08 to $158.5 million in 2018–191, p. 23. The ACCC found that this falls more heavily on people in higher-risk areas, and considered that it unfairly penalises them1, p. 23.

Tax rates have changed little. Stamp duty in Western Australia and the Northern Territory stayed at 10% over the 12 years to 2018–19. In Queensland, it rose from 7.5% to 9% on 31 July 20131, p. 54. In Tasmania, it rose from 8% to 10% on 1 October 20127.

The NSW levy has grown. The NSW Government reports that the cost of the Emergency Services Levy on residential insurance rose 48% from 2017–18 to 2023–2410.

Other costs drive the underlying premium. The ACCC found that the main driver of higher technical premiums in northern Australia was higher natural peril risk1, p. 60. Why premiums change sets out the other cost pressures.

What reviews have recommended

ACCC, 2020. The ACCC recommended that Western Australia, the Northern Territory and Queensland abolish stamp duty on home, contents and strata insurance. It noted that stamp duties on insurance are widely acknowledged as an inefficient form of taxation1, p. 57. If the duty is kept, the ACCC recommended basing it on the sum insured. It estimated that most postcodes in northern Australia would then pay less duty1, pp. 57-59. The ACCC also noted that the 2010 Review of Australia’s Future Tax System recommended abolishing all specific taxes on insurance products1, pp. 56-57.

NSW Parliament, 2026. In November 2026, a NSW parliamentary committee recommended that the next emergency services levy be decoupled from insurance premiums, to reduce home insurance premiums and affordability stress. It found no clear consensus on which of NSW Treasury’s five replacement options to adopt, and recommended that the next Parliament adopt a new funding source9, p. 8. The government response is due by 18 May 20279.

Reforms in place. The ACT abolished insurance duty on 1 July 20168. Victoria is reducing duty on business insurance by 1% a year until it is abolished from 1 July 2033. Duty on general insurance policies remains at 10%3.

Who regulates home and car insurance in Australia?

The regulators and laws behind home and car insurance, who sets premiums, and where to go if something goes wrong.

This page gives general information. It is not legal or financial product advice and does not take your circumstances into account.

Short answer

APRA supervises insurers so that they can meet their obligations to customers. ASIC regulates financial services, including how insurance claims are handled. AFCA handles complaints about insurers for free. Insurers set home and car insurance premiums. Every state and territory requires compulsory third party (CTP) insurance. In NSW, the state regulator SIRA licenses CTP insurers and reviews their premiums.

The main bodies

Not all of these bodies are regulators.
Body What it does
Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA) Supervises banks, insurers and a large part of the superannuation industry. Its primary mandate is to make sure these institutions, including general, life and private health insurers, have enough financial means to meet their obligations to customers (APRA 2026b).
Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC) Regulates corporations, markets, financial services and consumer credit (ASIC 2026). Insurers, and certain other businesses that handle insurance claims for them, need an ASIC licence to handle and settle claims (ASIC 2025).
Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) Enforces the Competition and Consumer Act 2010 and other laws that promote competition and fair trading (ACCC 2026a). At the government’s request, it ran an inquiry into insurance in northern Australia from 2017 to 2020 (ACCC 2020). It then monitored insurance prices, costs and profits around the cyclone reinsurance pool, reporting from 2022 to June 2026 (ACCC 2026b).
Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA) Hears complaints about insurance and other financial services, free of charge. It is an independent dispute resolution scheme (Moneysmart 2026).
Australian Reinsurance Pool Corporation (ARPC) A public financial corporation set up under the Terrorism and Cyclone Insurance Act 2003. It runs the cyclone reinsurance pool, which provides reinsurance for cyclone and cyclone-related flood damage to homes, small businesses and strata properties (ARPC 2026). It is not a regulator.
State Insurance Regulatory Authority (SIRA), NSW Licenses the insurers in the NSW CTP scheme (SIRA 2026a).

The main laws

Insurance Act 1973. APRA’s prudential standards for general insurers are made under this Act. One example is the capital adequacy standard, which requires general insurers to hold adequate capital against the risks of their activities (APRA 2023).

Insurance Contracts Act 1984. This Act governs insurance contracts. Section 13 states that a contract of insurance is based on the utmost good faith, a duty owed by both the insurer and the customer (Commonwealth of Australia 1984).

Corporations Act 2001. A business that provides financial services in Australia generally needs an Australian financial services (AFS) licence. Since 1 January 2022, insurers have needed a licence to handle and settle insurance claims, unless an exemption applies (ASIC 2025).

CTP laws. CTP insurance is compulsory in all Australian states and territories and is required to register a vehicle. In NSW the scheme runs under the Motor Accident Injuries Act 2017 (SIRA 2026a).

Who sets premiums

Insurers set their own home and car insurance premiums. For home insurance, the ACCC’s 2020 inquiry into insurance in northern Australia describes how an insurer builds a technical premium from expected claims costs, reinsurance costs, operating costs, commissions, and a margin for profit or return on capital. The insurer then adjusts it and adds discounts, surcharges, taxes, duties and levies to reach the price a customer pays (ACCC 2020, 60–61). ASIC has said that it cannot set insurance premiums, but it can hold insurers accountable for their pricing practices (Kirkland 2025).

CTP premiums work differently in NSW. Insurers file their CTP premiums with SIRA, and filed premiums must be adequate and not excessive. SIRA may reject a premium it considers excessive or inadequate in relation to actuarial advice and other financial information (SIRA 2026b).

If something goes wrong

Complaints. Contact the insurer first. If that does not fix the problem, make a formal written complaint to the insurer. If the insurer does not resolve it, or does not respond within a reasonable time, you can complain to AFCA (Moneysmart 2026).

If an insurer fails. The Australian Government can activate the Financial Claims Scheme if a general insurer fails. APRA then assesses each claim and decides the amount payable. Most policyholders are covered for valid claims under $5,000. Individuals who are Australian citizens or permanent residents, and Australian small businesses, not-for-profits and family trusts, are also covered for valid claims of $5,000 and above. The scheme does not apply to life or private health insurers (APRA 2026a).

Who regulates home and car insurance in Australia?

The regulators and laws behind home and car insurance, who sets premiums, and where to go if something goes wrong.

This page gives general information. It is not legal or financial product advice and does not take your circumstances into account.

Short answer

APRA supervises insurers so that they can meet their obligations to customers. ASIC regulates financial services, including how insurance claims are handled. AFCA handles complaints about insurers for free. Insurers set home and car insurance premiums. Every state and territory requires compulsory third party (CTP) insurance and runs its own scheme.

The main bodies

Body What it does
Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA) Australia’s financial system prudential regulator1. It supervises banks, insurers and a large part of the superannuation industry. Its primary mandate is to make sure these institutions, including general, life and private health insurers, have enough financial means to meet their obligations to customers2. Its standards for insurers cover areas such as capital and risk management3; 4.
Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC) Australia’s financial markets conduct regulator1. It regulates corporations, markets, financial services and consumer credit5. For insurance, its work includes licensing and claims handling, misleading conduct, unfair contract terms, product design and distribution, and the accuracy of what insurers tell customers about pricing6–10.
Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) Enforces the Competition and Consumer Act 2010 and other laws that promote competition and fair trading11. At the government’s request, it ran an inquiry into insurance in northern Australia from 2017 to 202012. It then monitored insurance prices, costs and profits around the cyclone reinsurance pool, reporting from 2022 to June 202613.
Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA) Hears complaints about insurance and other financial services, free of charge. It is an independent dispute resolution scheme14.
Australian Reinsurance Pool Corporation (ARPC) A public financial corporation set up under the Terrorism and Cyclone Insurance Act 2003. It runs the cyclone reinsurance pool, which provides reinsurance for cyclone and cyclone-related flood damage to homes, small businesses and strata properties15. It is not a regulator.
State and territory CTP bodies Each state and territory runs its own CTP scheme. Some license and supervise private CTP insurers, such as the State Insurance Regulatory Authority (SIRA) in NSW16 and the Motor Accident Insurance Commission in Queensland17. Others provide CTP through a government-owned body, such as the Transport Accident Commission in Victoria18.

The main laws

Insurance Act 1973. APRA’s prudential standards for general insurers are made under this Act. One example is the capital adequacy standard, which requires general insurers to hold adequate capital against the risks of their activities3. The Act also sets up the Financial Claims Scheme for policyholders of general insurers that fail19. See If something goes wrong below.

Insurance Contracts Act 1984. This Act governs insurance contracts. Section 13 states that a contract of insurance is based on the utmost good faith, a duty owed by both the insurer and the customer20. Under section 20B, a customer buying consumer insurance has a duty to take reasonable care not to make a misrepresentation to the insurer before the contract is entered into. Section 35 and the Insurance Contracts Regulations 2017 set a standard level of cover for some types of policy, including motor vehicle, home building and home contents insurance. An insurer can offer less than the standard cover only if it clearly informs the customer in writing before the contract is entered into21; 22.

Corporations Act 2001. A business that provides financial services in Australia generally needs an Australian financial services (AFS) licence. Since 1 January 2022, insurers have needed a licence to handle and settle insurance claims, unless an exemption applies6. Since 5 October 2021, the design and distribution obligations have required firms to design financial products to meet the needs of consumers. From the same date, add-on insurance has been subject to a deferred sales model, with a four-day pause between the sale of a main product and the sale of add-on insurance9.

ASIC Act 2001. The ASIC Act and the Corporations Act prohibit false or misleading statements and misleading or deceptive conduct in relation to financial products and services, including insurance7. The ASIC Act also contains the unfair contract terms protections8. They apply to consumer and small business insurance contracts entered into or renewed from 5 April 202123. A term found to be unfair is void, which means it is not binding8.

Terrorism and Cyclone Insurance Act 2003. The Australian Reinsurance Pool Corporation is set up under this Act and runs the cyclone reinsurance pool15.

CTP laws. CTP insurance is compulsory in every state and territory and is required to register a vehicle16. Each state and territory runs its own scheme under its own law. For example:

  • In NSW, insurers licensed by SIRA provide CTP under the Motor Accident Injuries Act 201716.
  • In Queensland, the Motor Accident Insurance Commission licenses and supervises CTP insurers. It was set up under the Motor Accident Insurance Act 199417.
  • In Victoria, the Transport Accident Commission, which is owned by the Victorian Government, pays for treatment and benefits for people injured in transport accidents18.

How CTP premiums are set is covered under Who sets premiums.

Industry code

The Insurance Council of Australia’s General Insurance Code of Practice is an industry code. The current Code was last updated in October 202324. In October 2026 the Insurance Council submitted a revised Code to ASIC for approval. Under the revised Code, the Code’s obligations would become part of consumer insurance contracts, so customers could enforce them directly. A transition period is expected before it takes effect on new and renewed policies25.

Reforms under way

In the 2026–27 Budget, the Government provided $2.4 million over two years for Treasury to develop and consult on options to make the basis of home and contents premiums clearer. The same funding covers legislating standard definitions of natural hazard terms used in property insurance contracts26.

Who sets premiums

Insurers set their own home and car insurance premiums. For home insurance, the ACCC’s 2020 inquiry into insurance in northern Australia describes how an insurer builds a technical premium from expected claims costs, reinsurance costs, operating costs, commissions, and a margin for profit or return on capital. The insurer then adjusts it and adds discounts, surcharges, taxes, duties and levies to reach the price a customer pays12, pp. 60-61. ASIC has said that it cannot set insurance premiums, but it can hold insurers accountable for their pricing practices10.

CTP premiums are set differently in each state and territory. For example:

  • In NSW, insurers file their premiums with SIRA. Filed premiums must be adequate and not excessive, and SIRA may reject a premium it considers excessive or inadequate in relation to actuarial advice and other financial information27.
  • In Queensland, insurers set premiums for each vehicle class every quarter, within maximum and minimum limits set by MAIC. The premium does not depend on the driver’s age or driving record, or on the vehicle’s age or location28.
  • In the ACT, the scheme is community rated. Motorists in each vehicle class pay the same premium, regardless of their individual circumstances and risk of being involved in an accident29. All premiums need approval by the MAI Commission30.
  • In Victoria, the TAC charge is paid with vehicle registration. It depends on the type and use of the vehicle and the risk zone of the postcode where it is kept31.

If something goes wrong

Complaints. Contact the insurer first. If that does not fix the problem, make a formal written complaint to the insurer. If the insurer does not resolve it, or does not respond within a reasonable time, you can complain to AFCA14.

If an insurer fails. The Australian Government can activate the Financial Claims Scheme if a general insurer fails. APRA then assesses each claim and decides the amount payable. Most policyholders are covered for valid claims under $5,000. Individuals who are Australian citizens or permanent residents, and Australian small businesses, not-for-profits and family trusts, are also covered for valid claims of $5,000 and above. The scheme does not apply to life or private health insurers32.

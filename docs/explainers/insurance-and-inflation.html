How does insurance contribute to inflation?

How the ABS measures insurance prices, insurance’s weight in the CPI, and how much it adds to inflation.

Short answer

Insurance makes up 1.36% of the Consumer Price Index basket. In the year to September 2026, insurance prices rose 5.4% and added 0.07 percentage points to the 4.3% rise in the CPI.

How the CPI measures insurance

The CPI combines two pieces of information for each item: how much its price changed, and its weight in household spending.

Price. The insurance expenditure class covers comprehensive insurance for dwellings, home contents and motor vehicles, and compulsory third party motor vehicle insurance. Health insurance is classified in the Health group. The ABS measures price change using gross premiums, collected each month from web-scraped price quotes for a range of customer profiles. The ABS does not publish separate indexes for home and car insurance1.

Weight. The CPI weights insurance by the cost of the service provided by insurers, which the ABS measures as gross premiums less claims. The ABS updates the weights once a year, using National Accounts data, and holds them fixed between updates1.

How much insurance adds to inflation

At the December quarter 2024 reweighting, insurance accounted for 1.36% of the all-groups CPI. In the year to September 2026, it contributed 0.07 percentage points of the 4.3% annual rise. The ABS calculates and publishes this contribution figure2.

The Selected Living Cost Indexes

The ABS’s Selected Living Cost Indexes weight insurance by the gross premiums households pay3. The CPI uses premiums less claims.

Taxes on premiums

When the GST was introduced in 2000, the CPI insurance index rose about 30% in a year. The premiums households paid rose much less. At the time, the CPI valued insurance at premiums less claims, which is much smaller than the premium. The GST was charged on the full premium, so the extra tax was a far larger share of the CPI’s value than of the premium. In an ABS example, a rise in tax from 5% to 15% lifted the premium by 9.5% but the CPI’s value by 22.2%4.

What types of insurance do households buy?

The main types of insurance in Australia, what each covers, common limits, and where to find official guidance.

This page gives general information. It is not financial product advice and does not take your circumstances into account.

Short answer

Most households insure their home, their belongings and their car. These are home building, home contents and car insurance. Every registered car must also have compulsory third party (CTP) insurance. This site focuses on these types. This page also gives a short introduction to life, health, travel, pet, landlord and business insurance, and to annuities for retirement income.

Home insurance

Source: (Moneysmart 2026c, 2026f).
Type What it covers
Home building Repairing or replacing the house when something goes wrong. It usually also covers fixtures and other structures on the property.
Home contents Replacing household items and belongings if they are damaged, lost or stolen.

Home building and contents cover can often be bundled into one policy. Renters do not need home building insurance, because insuring the building is the landlord’s responsibility. In strata properties such as apartments, the body corporate may be responsible for insuring the building. Owners are still responsible for insuring their own belongings. Renters can choose to take out contents insurance (Moneysmart 2026c).

Car insurance

Source: (Moneysmart 2026b).
Type What it covers
Compulsory third party (CTP) Injuries to people caused by your car. It does not pay for damage to cars or property.
Third party property damage Damage your car causes to other people’s cars or property. It does not cover your own car.
Third party property, fire and theft The same as third party property damage, plus your own car if it is stolen or damaged by fire.
Comprehensive Damage to your car and to other people’s cars or property, even if you caused the accident. It also covers theft, fire and weather events.

CTP is the only compulsory type of car insurance. You need it to register a car, and in some states it is included in the registration cost (Moneysmart 2026b).

Life insurance

Source: (Moneysmart 2026f).
Type What it covers
Life cover Generally pays a lump sum to your loved ones if you die.
Total and permanent disability (TPD) A safety net if illness or injury means you can never work again.
Trauma A lump sum if you are diagnosed with a serious illness or are injured.
Income protection Replaces your income, or part of it, while you recover from illness or injury.

Most super funds offer life, TPD and income protection insurance to their members. Premiums are deducted from the super balance, which reduces retirement savings (Moneysmart 2026g).

Life insurance companies also sell annuities, which pay regular income in retirement for a set number of years or for the rest of your life (Moneysmart 2026a). What are annuities? explains how they work.

For income protection, insurers usually ask about age, job, income, medical and family health history, smoking and lifestyle, and high-risk hobbies or sports. This information helps them decide whether to insure you, the premium and the policy terms. Income protection usually replaces a percentage of pre-tax income, such as 75% or 90%. Most policies have a waiting period of between 14 days and two years before payments start, and pay for two or five years, or up to a set age such as 65 (Moneysmart 2026e).

Health insurance

Private health insurance can help pay hospital and medical costs not covered by Medicare. There are three main types. Ambulance cover pays for emergency ambulance transport. Hospital cover helps with some of the costs of staying in hospital. Extras cover pays for out-of-hospital treatment such as physiotherapy, optical and dental (Moneysmart 2026d).

Unlike home and car insurance, which are risk rated, private health insurance is community rated. Everyone pays the same price for a particular policy, an insurer cannot refuse to insure you or sell you the policy you want, and you are guaranteed the right to renew (Department of Health, Disability and Ageing 2026).

Most policies have waiting periods before you can claim. Premiums change each year on 1 April, and the average increase in 2026 was 4.41%. People without private health insurance who earn above a threshold may pay the Medicare levy surcharge of up to 1.5% of taxable income (Moneysmart 2026d). People who first take out hospital cover after the 1 July following their 31st birthday pay a Lifetime Health Cover loading of 2% of the hospital premium for each year they are over 30, up to 70%. The loading is removed after 10 years of continuous hospital cover (privatehealth.gov.au 2026).

Travel insurance

Most travel insurance policies cover medical costs, cancellations and delays, lost or stolen baggage, and car hire. Exclusions often apply to high-risk activities, illness or injury from pre-existing medical conditions, travel to areas with an official travel warning, and pandemics. If the government’s smartraveller advice for a destination is “do not travel”, a policy usually will not cover you. Some credit cards include travel insurance, which may cover only the cardholder and only trips paid for on the card (Moneysmart 2026i).

Pet insurance

Pet insurance can be accident-only, accident and illness, or comprehensive, which also covers some routine care such as vaccinations. Most insurers do not cover pre-existing conditions, most policies have waiting periods, and policies can have annual limits on benefits. Most insurers require you to pay the vet in full and then make a claim (Moneysmart 2026h).

Landlord insurance

Insuring the building of a rental property is the landlord’s responsibility, while tenants insure their own belongings (Moneysmart 2026c). What landlord policies cover varies, so the product disclosure statement is the main guide.

Business insurance

Businesses may need some insurance by law, and other types are a choice. Employers must get workers compensation insurance from an authorised insurer, and the rules vary by state and territory (business.gov.au 2026). In New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland and South Australia, a single public insurer performs most or all of the insurer’s functions. In Western Australia, Tasmania, the Northern Territory and the ACT private sector, cover comes from approved private insurers (Safe Work Australia 2023). Other common types of business insurance include public liability insurance, which covers injury or property damage caused by the business’s negligence, building and contents insurance, business interruption insurance, which pays ongoing costs if an insured event such as a fire interrupts the business, and cyber insurance (business.gov.au 2026).

Other types

Moneysmart also covers mobile phone and funeral insurance (Moneysmart 2026f). Its insurance guides explain how to choose and compare each type.

What this site covers

The ABS insurance price index covers comprehensive insurance for dwellings, home contents and motor vehicles, and compulsory third party insurance. Health insurance is in the Health group of the CPI, not in insurance (ABS 2025). The figures and explainers on this site therefore focus on home and car insurance. The other types on this page are introduced briefly, with links to official guidance.

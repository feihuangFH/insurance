What this shows

How prices for comparable insurance policies, such as home, contents and motor vehicle insurance, have changed. The ABS prices the same kinds of policy over time, so the index tracks price change rather than spending.

What it does not show

What households pay in total, which also moves with sums insured, coverage choices and risk. It also does not show why prices changed. Why premiums change sets out the drivers and the evidence reported by regulators and industry bodies.

Source: ABS, Consumer Price Index, Australia (Data API, dataflow CPI). Quarterly, original series, Australia. Annual and cumulative changes calculated from index numbers. Retrieved 3 November 2026. Download data (CSV)

Why premiums change

Why are insurance premiums increasing?

Actuaries start from the expected cost of claims and add the costs of running the business and holding capital. The market price can then move away from this technical price.

Expected claims How often claims occur and what they cost + Expenses Administration, distribution, compliance + Risk and capital loading Reinsurance and the cost of capital = Technical premium The actuarially calculated price

The premium a customer pays can also reflect competition, pricing strategy, taxes such as stamp duty and GST, and levies.

01

Claims costs

Repair and rebuilding costs, labour, materials, vehicle parts and replacement values.

02

Natural disasters and climate risk

Floods, bushfires, cyclones and storms, and how insurers assess hazard, exposure and vulnerability at each location.

03

Reinsurance

Insurers pass part of their catastrophe risk to reinsurers. The cost of reinsurance is part of the premium.

04

Operating and capital costs

Administration, distribution, regulation and the return investors require on the capital insurers must hold.

05

Competition and pricing strategy

Market conditions, customer behaviour and pricing practices such as price optimisation.

06

Data and risk modelling

The data and models insurers use to estimate each customer’s risk.
